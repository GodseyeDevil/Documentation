Urumi- Flatbed Cutter

The project started with inspiration from a machineat Fab Lab Zund. It is a versatile machine with three modules and a set of tools that allow you to perform a lot of operations, such as cutting, creasing, routing, and many more. The objective is to make a machine like that, but open source, and build it as a modular platform where we can add different sets of tools and upgrade the machine from there. The machine is designed to be upgradeable, and nothing is constant. The machine is expected to change and evolve every now and then—that is the idea.

The designed machine had some constraints during the design process. The machine needed to have stable kinematics. Even though CoreXY is one of the most stable kinematic systems we know, we were hesitant to use it because CoreXY would create a large belt loop, increasing the possibility of belt slack. So, we went with a Cartesian system. To balance the system, both ends of the Y-axis were connected with a rod so that the entire gantry would move in sync.

The X-axis is relatively simple, with a motor connected directly to the linear motion system. There is only one moving assembly, so there are no complexities like those in the Y-axis. It is a simple system.

Now comes the X-axis gantry and the A-axis. The combination is a funny one. The design is such that the mass of the A-axis motor is positioned at the back of the gantry instead of at the front. This is because the motor increases the moment on the gantry, so it was moved to the back. The ZA-axis also has a specific assembly because, without it, constraining everything within the tight available space would be very difficult. It is a preventive measure to reduce cantilevered mass and improve stability. Also, I am done with cantilever designs.

That is the mechanism for the machine. Now comes the modifications, such as adding a stopper switch for the A-axis, calibrating the belt tension, and making all the other necessary adjustments.

It was fun designing it.
